## Key Takeaways (for content strategy)

1. **Growth isn't steady — it's one hit plus a slow baseline.** Strip out
   the Aug 18–20 viral window and TikTok's real daily average is 15K–100K
   views, not the inflated 4.16M/28-day figure implies.
2. **You're being watched and shared, not talked to.** Comment-to-like
   ratio is ~0.3–0.4% on both platforms despite huge share/save numbers.
   People pass your content along; they don't stop to type. If building a
   community/coaching business matters, ending videos with a direct,
   specific question is the lever most likely to move this.
3. **Evergreen wins are your psychology content, not your memes.** Videos
   tagged `#zaetalks #selfimprovement` are still earning views 6+ months
   after posting. Prank/relatable-meme content spikes fast and dies fast.
   This validates leaning further into the teaching format.
4. **Your audience skews male 72–82%**, mostly 18–34. Worth checking
   whether that's who you want buying UNMONITORED/coaching, or whether
   framing/examples are unintentionally coding male-default.
5. **Your best "brand story" content underperforms your best "brand
   growth" content.** The exposure-ladder essay post (your most authentic,
   mission-aligned content) got 1,647 views; a low-effort viral post got
   3.7M. Growth and mission-alignment aren't the same lever — decide
   deliberately which one a given post is optimizing for.
6. **What's not measured yet:** click-through from bio link to actual guide
   downloads. Views and engagement are vanity until they convert — a
   trackable link (Linktree analytics, or distinct short links per
   platform) would answer "which platform's audience actually acts."
